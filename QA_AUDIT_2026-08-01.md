## Executive Summary

**Overall risk rating: CRITICAL. Not safe for production as the book of record.**

The engineering quality here is genuinely above average for a system this size. Idempotency is thought about, the IPN receiver is well defended, double-entry postings balance, posting failures are captured durably instead of swallowed, and the existing suite (354 tests) passes clean. The problems are not sloppiness — they are a small number of **modelling** mistakes that are individually subtle and collectively fatal to the books.

The system currently has **two disagreeing definitions of "what a tenant owes"** (the `Arrears` model vs. the statement ledger), **two disagreeing definitions of income** (payment-derived vs. ledger-derived), and **no way to represent a credit balance**. Six defects were confirmed by execution, not inference.

The single most urgent item is not an accounting nuance: a manually-entered payment can be **silently discarded** and the API still returns `201 Created` with another tenant's record.

## Critical Issues

### C1 — A manual payment can be silently swallowed and credited to the wrong tenant
**Severity:** Critical · **Likelihood:** High · **Priority:** P0

**Location:** `backend/apps/payments/services.py:76-79`, `backend/apps/payments/views.py:112`

`Payment.idempotency_key` is enforced by a **global** partial-unique constraint, and `perform_create` sets `idempotency_key = reference`. `process_payment` then does:

```python
if idempotency_key:
    existing = Payment.objects.filter(idempotency_key=idempotency_key).first()
    if existing:
        return existing   # no check that tenant / amount / date match
```

The returned payment is not validated against the incoming one. Two different tenants using the same reference string collapse into one record.

M-Pesa `TransID`s are globally unique, so the IPN path is safe. **The manual path is not**: cash receipt books restart at `001`, cheque numbers repeat across banks, deposit-slip numbers repeat per branch. Staff entering `reference="001"` for tenant B after tenant A used `001` will receive `HTTP 201` with **tenant A's payment body**, while tenant B's cash is never recorded and they remain in arrears — and are then dunned by SMS for money they paid.

**Business impact:** Missing revenue, false arrears, tenant disputes, and an unfalsifiable "we never received it" position. Silent — no error, no log, no alert.

**Fix:**
1. Scope the key: `idempotency_key = f"{tenant.id}:{source}:{reference}"` and make the DB constraint `(tenant, idempotency_key)`.
2. On a hit, verify `tenant_id`, `amount`, and `payment_date` match; if they differ, raise `409 Conflict` rather than returning the other record.
3. Return `200` (not `201`) on a genuine idempotent replay so the client can tell the difference.

### C2 — Commercial VAT breaks arrears, FIFO allocation, and the statement
**Severity:** Critical · **Likelihood:** Certain (every commercial tenant, every month) · **Priority:** P0

**Location:** `backend/apps/payments/services.py:253-305` (`_update_arrears`), `services.py:157-250` (`allocate_payment_fifo`), `backend/apps/payments/statement_service.py:101-149`

Two incompatible conventions meet with no conversion:

| Quantity | Convention | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| `Tenant.monthly_rent` / `Arrears.expected_rent` | **VAT-exclusive** (base) | `import_matasia.py:173` — "Commercial stores BASE rent (J); the engine adds 16% VAT" |
| `Payment.amount` | **VAT-inclusive** (gross cash) | `services.py:124-138` — "`Payment.amount` stays the gross received" |

`_update_arrears` compares them directly: `balance = max(expected_rent - total_paid, 0)`.

**Proven behaviour** (`test_f1`): a commercial tenant owing 24,000 base pays the correct 27,840 gross. Result: `expected_rent=24,000`, `amount_paid=27,840`, `balance=0`, `is_cleared=True`. The 3,840 owed to KRA is recorded as rent overpayment.

**Proven behaviour** (`test_f1b`): FIFO sizes each chunk against the VAT-exclusive `Arrears.balance`. A 27,840 credit against a 24,000 May arrear produces two Payments — 24,000 to May and **3,840 booked as June rent**. The VAT becomes next month's rental income, in the wrong period, on the wrong tenant obligation.

Meanwhile `statement_service._build_ledger` charges rent **and** a separate "16% VAT on Rent" line, then credits gross payments — so the statement's running balance is correct while `Arrears.balance` says zero. **The two figures the business quotes to a tenant permanently disagree.**

**Business impact:** VAT liability under-recognised; commercial arrears understated by 13.8% of every payment; period allocation wrong; KRA exposure; the arrears report and the tenant statement contradict each other.

**Fix:** Normalise at the boundary. Either (a) store `Arrears.expected_rent` gross for BUSINESS units and add an `expected_vat` column, or (b) split incoming commercial cash before allocation and settle `expected_rent` against the **net** portion only, routing VAT to a separate liability tracker. Option (b) matches the existing `split_tax_inclusive` design. Add a reconciliation test asserting `Arrears.balance == statement_total_due` for a BUSINESS tenant.

### C3 — Voiding a commercial payment never reaches the ledger; voiding a deposit corrupts it
**Severity:** Critical · **Likelihood:** Medium (every authorised reversal on a commercial unit) · **Priority:** P0

**Location:** `backend/apps/payments/admin.py:35-50` (`_void_payment`), `backend/apps/ledger/posting.py:198-263`, `backend/apps/payments/tax_service.py:110-111`

`_void_payment` creates a compensating `Payment` with a **negative** amount. The `post_save` signal calls `post_payment`, which for a BUSINESS unit calls `_split_vat_inclusive(-27840)` → `split_tax_inclusive` → `ValueError: gross_amount must be positive`. `_safe_post` catches it and writes a `PostingFailure` row.

**Proven behaviour** (`test_f4`): the void `Payment` row is created, **no `JournalEntry` exists for it**, and an unresolved `PostingFailure` is left behind. The director clicks "Authorize reversal", sees a success message, arrears are corrected — **and the general ledger still shows the original income forever.**

Two compounding defects in the same eight lines:
- `_void_payment` **omits `payment_type`**, so it defaults to `RENT`. Voiding a `DEPOSIT` posts against 1020/4110 (bank & rental income) instead of 1030/2100 (deposit bank & deposit liability) — corrupting four accounts at once.
- No `Transaction` row is created for the void, so the `Transaction` table (documented as *the* auditable financial record) permanently disagrees with `Payment`.

**Fix:** Do not model voids as negative `Payment` rows. Add an explicit `reversed_by` / `is_void` relationship and call the existing, correct `reverse_payment()` posting function. If negative payments are retained, carry `payment_type` forward and make `posting.py` handle negative amounts by sign-flipping the legs rather than routing through the VAT splitter. Add a monitored alert on any unresolved `PostingFailure`.

### C4 — Security deposits clear rent arrears
**Severity:** Critical · **Likelihood:** High · **Priority:** P0

**Location:** `backend/apps/payments/services.py:260-284`

```python
total_paid = Payment.objects.filter(
    tenant=tenant, period_month=..., period_year=...
).aggregate(total=models.Sum("amount"))["total"]
```

No `payment_type` filter. Every payment type — `DEPOSIT`, `LATE_FEE`, `OTHER` — counts toward clearing rent.

**Proven behaviour** (`test_f2`): a 15,000 security deposit against a 15,000 rent obligation sets `balance=0, is_cleared=True`. The tenant has paid **no rent** but shows as fully paid, receives no reminder, and appears nowhere in the arrears report.

The reporting layer already gets this right (`views_reports.py:20` — `INCOME_PAYMENT_FILTER = ~Q(payment_type=DEPOSIT)`) and the ledger books deposits to 2100 correctly. So the P&L says "no rental income", the ledger says "liability held", and `Arrears` says "rent paid". Three sources, three answers.

**Fix:** Filter `_update_arrears` to rent-settling types only. Apply the same filter in `recalculate_all_statuses` (`tasks.py:337`) and `recalculate_unit_status`. This also affects `get_collection_progress` (`services.py:318`).

### C5 — No authorization model whatsoever
**Severity:** Critical · **Likelihood:** Certain · **Priority:** P0

**Location:** `backend/apps/accounts/models.py:14-27`; every viewset

`User(AbstractUser)` adds only a unique email. **There is no role, no permission class, no object-level scoping anywhere in the codebase** (`find apps -name permissions.py` returns nothing). Every endpoint is `permission_classes = [IsAuthenticated]`.

Any authenticated account — a caretaker, a data-entry clerk, a compromised session — can:
- read every payment, transaction, tenant record, KRA PIN and ID number in the portfolio (no pagination, so one request returns everything);
- **create payments** (`POST /api/payments/`);
- **waive arrears** (`POST /api/payments/arrears/{id}/waive/`) — permanently writes off debt, no approval, no maker-checker;
- **assign unmatched bank credits to any tenant** (`POST /api/unmatched-credits/{id}/assign/`);
- **mint money** via `/api/payments/mock/` (see C6).

The only maker-checker in the system is on reversals in Django admin (`admin.py:184-202`), and it is bypassable via the API path.

**Business impact:** Complete absence of segregation of duties. This alone fails any audit. Broken Object-Level Authorization across the entire API.

**Fix:** Add a `role` field (`OWNER` / `ACCOUNTANT` / `CARETAKER` / `VIEWER`), write DRF permission classes, and gate write endpoints — especially `waive`, `assign`, and payment creation — behind explicit roles. Add building-level scoping if caretakers are per-property.

### C6 — `/api/payments/mock/` fabricates real financial records in production
**Severity:** Critical · **Likelihood:** High · **Priority:** P0

**Location:** `backend/apps/payments/views.py:129-165`

The endpoint is documented as a simulator but "runs the full processing pipeline". It is gated only on `IsAuthenticated` — **no `DEBUG` check, no staff check, no environment guard.**

**Proven behaviour** (`test_f6`): a non-staff, non-superuser account posts `{"tenant": 1, "amount": "500000", "source": "mpesa"}` and receives `201`. A real `Payment`, a real `Transaction`, a real `JournalEntry` crediting 4110 Residential Rental Income, and cleared arrears all result — from money that does not exist.

**Fix:** Delete the endpoint, or gate it on `settings.DEBUG` and refuse to register the route when `DEBUG is False`.

## High Issues

### H1 — Overpayments and prepayments are destroyed
**Location:** `services.py:283` · `balance = max(expected_rent - covered, Decimal("0"))`

There is no credit-balance concept anywhere in the schema. A tenant who prepays three months has the excess floored to zero.

**Proven** (`test_f5`): a 25,000 payment against 10,000 rent leaves `amount_paid=25,000, balance=0`. `generate_monthly_arrears` then raises next month at the **full** 10,000, ignoring the 15,000 credit. The tenant is marked in arrears and receives an overdue SMS having paid 2½ months ahead.

`allocate_payment_fifo` only spills forward within a *single* transaction; it cannot consume a credit from a prior one. **Fix:** add a `TenantCredit` ledger (or allow negative `Arrears.balance` with explicit carry-forward) and consume it in `generate_monthly_arrears`.

### H2 — Waivers are invisible to the tenant statement
**Location:** `statement_service.py:108-121`

`_build_ledger` raises a charge row for `arr.expected_rent` and never credits `arr.waived_amount`. Waiving an arrear zeroes `Arrears.balance` but the statement keeps showing the full charge — so the PDF the tenant receives shows debt the business has already written off, permanently.

### H3 — Deposits double-count on the statement
**Location:** `statement_service.py:128-131` and `:224-227`

Every `Payment` — including `DEPOSIT` type — is credited into the rent running balance as "Payment Received", *and* the same deposits are summed separately into the "Security Deposit" breakdown line. The deposit both reduces rent owed and is presented as a held liability.

### H4 — Accounts Receivable is never relieved
**Location:** `posting.py:493-520` (`post_utility_charge`), `posting.py:568-628` (`post_opening_balances`), `posting.py:395-408` (policy note)

The system is deliberately cash-basis for rent — rent hits the GL only on receipt, and `post_arrear` is intentionally unwired. That is coherent. But `post_utility_charge` posts **DR 1040 / CR 4150**, raising a receivable, and `post_opening_balances` posts **DR 1040 / CR 3300**. When the tenant pays, `post_payment` credits *income* (4110/4120/4150), never 1040.

**1040 Accounts Receivable therefore only ever increases.** The balance sheet asset is fictitious and grows without bound; utility income is recognised twice (once on billing to 4150, once on collection to 4150).

### H5 — Reversal detection is a bounded substring scan
**Location:** `coop_ipn.py:249-269`

```python
for ev in CoopIpnEvent.objects.filter(status=RECORDED).only(...)[:500]:
    if (ev.payment_ref and ev.payment_ref in text) or (ev.transaction_id in text):
```

Two failure modes: (a) the queryset is ordered `-received_at`, so a reversal of a payment older than the most recent 500 events **is never linked** to its original; (b) matching is unanchored substring — a short `payment_ref` like `"1234"` will match any narration containing those digits, linking a reversal to the **wrong** payment. Since authorising the reversal voids `event.payment`, a false positive voids an innocent tenant's payment.

Also: `_reversal_check` runs an unbounded-ish scan **inside** the IPN request's atomic block, extending the transaction and the bank's wait.

### H6 — Webhook authentication is a static shared secret with no signature or replay window
**Location:** `coop_ipn.py:113-158`, `config/settings/base.py:236-249`

Well-built for what it does — timing-safe compare, fail-closed, IP allowlist with correct `X-Forwarded-For` handling, per-IP throttle. But:
- **No HMAC payload signature.** Anyone holding the bearer token can post arbitrary credits that auto-create Payments and GL income for any unit label they guess.
- **No timestamp / freshness check.** Replay is blocked only by the `TransactionId` unique constraint — a *modified* replay with a fresh ID is accepted.
- **`COOP_IPN_ALLOWED_IPS` defaults to empty = allow all.** Per the code comment this is intentional pending Co-op sharing their range; in production it means the token is the only control.
- No token rotation mechanism.

Combined with C6 and C5, there are three independent paths to injecting fictitious income.

### H7 — `Payment` is documented as immutable but is freely editable, with no audit trail
**Location:** `payments/models.py:1-6` vs `admin.py:81-89`, `ledger/signals.py:85-93`

The module docstring states payments are "never soft-deleted or modified". In practice `PaymentAdmin` exposes `amount`, `tenant`, `payment_date` as editable, and `on_payment_saved` explicitly supports rewriting the GL on edit (`replace=not created`). There is **no `created_by`, no `updated_by`, no change history** on `Payment`, `Arrears`, `Expense`, or `JournalEntry`.

An admin can change a payment amount; the ledger silently rewrites to match; nothing records who, when, or what the old value was. `LoginAttempt` gives login history (good) but no financial action is attributable. **This is an outright audit failure.**

### H8 — Celery runs eagerly in production, inside the bank's HTTP request
**Location:** `config/settings/production.py:70` · `CELERY_TASK_ALWAYS_EAGER = True`

Every `.delay()` executes inline. On the IPN path, `_safe_enqueue(send_deposit_receipt, …)` therefore performs statement construction, **PDF rendering**, an SMS API call, and an SMTP send **while Co-op's connection is open**. Multi-second latency per credit; on timeout Co-op re-delivers (idempotency holds, so no double-booking — but retry storms and eventual delivery-give-up are real). Same issue on `POST /api/payments/`.

## Database Findings

**Transactions & atomicity**
- ✅ `_process_payment_atomic` and `allocate_payment_fifo` are correctly atomic; the inner `@transaction.atomic` creates a savepoint so the `IntegrityError` catch in `process_payment` does not poison the outer transaction. This is subtle and done right.
- ✅ `select_for_update` on outstanding arrears correctly serialises concurrent credits for one tenant.
- ✅ The IPN "atomic claim" pattern (create `CoopIpnEvent` in the same transaction as the Payment) is the right design.
- ⚠ **`ledger.signals` writes `PostingFailure` inside the caller's transaction.** If the outer transaction later rolls back, the failure record rolls back with it — the very evidence of the problem is lost. Use `transaction.on_commit` or a separate connection.
- ⚠ `_reversal_check` performs a 500-row scan inside the IPN atomic block, lengthening the transaction under the bank's timeout.
- ⚠ Long-running work inside request transactions generally (statement build, PDF render) — see H8.

**Constraints**
- ✅ Good: unique `(source_type, source_id, kind)` on `JournalEntry`; unique `(tenant, month, year)` on `Arrears`; global `Upper(label)` uniqueness on `Unit`/`UnitAlias`; `JournalLine` check constraints preventing both-sides and zero-value lines; unique `transaction_id` on `Transaction` and `CoopIpnEvent`.
- ❌ `Payment.idempotency_key` unique is **global** rather than tenant-scoped (C1).
- ❌ No check constraint on `Payment.amount` (negative voids depend on this, which is itself the wrong design — see C3).
- ❌ No check constraint on `Arrears.balance >= 0` or `amount_paid >= 0`.
- ❌ No check constraint on `period_month BETWEEN 1 AND 12` at the DB level (serializer-only).
- ❌ No unique constraint on `UtilityCharge (tenant, period, label)` — the same water bill can be captured twice.
- ❌ No receipt-number model at all; "receipt number" is the M-Pesa reference, so there is no gapless, auditable receipt sequence.

**Indexes**
- ✅ Sensible coverage on `Payment`, `Transaction`, `CoopIpnEvent`, `JournalEntry`.
- ⚠ Missing: `Arrears(tenant, is_cleared)` — driving the tenant list annotation and arrears report; `UtilityCharge(posting_date)`; `Tenant(status)` is indexed but phone matching does a full scan in Python (`matching.py:209`).

**Concurrency**
- ✅ Duplicate webhook delivery — protected by unique `TransactionId` + atomic claim.
- ✅ Double-click "assign credit" — protected by `select_for_update` + status re-check.
- ✅ Double-click "authorize reversal" — same pattern, correct.
- ❌ Double-submit `POST /api/payments/` with a colliding reference — silently returns the wrong record (C1).
- ⚠ `generate_monthly_arrears` uses `get_or_create` (safe) but is triggerable concurrently via an unauthenticated-until-token HTTP endpoint running synchronously; two overlapping runs will contend.
- ⚠ `_update_arrears` reads `Payment` sums without locking the `Arrears` row; a payment landing concurrently with a waiver can compute a stale `total_paid`.

## Architecture Findings

**Maintainability**
- The `payments` app is doing too much: ingestion, matching, allocation, tax, statements, receipts, notifications, reconciliation, PDF, cron. `matching.py`, `tax_service.py`, `posting.py` are genuinely clean and well-documented; `views.py` and `admin.py` mix transport, business rules, and orchestration.
- **Duplicated logic:** the unmatched-credit assign flow is implemented twice — `views.py:364-416` (API) and `admin.py:294-375` (admin) — with **different safety properties**. The API version locks the row and re-checks status; the admin version does neither, so two admins assigning the same credit can double-book it. Neither passes an `idempotency_key` to `allocate_payment_fifo`, disabling the guard that path was built for.
- Business logic reachable through three doors (API viewset, Django admin action, management command) with divergent validation. Consolidate into a single service function.
- Heavy use of function-local imports to dodge circular dependencies — a signal that `payments` should be split (`ingestion` / `billing` / `notifications`).

**Scalability**
- No pagination anywhere (`REST_FRAMEWORK` has no `DEFAULT_PAGINATION_CLASS`). Every list endpoint returns the full table.
- `tenant_by_phone` (`matching.py:209`) loads and normalises **all active tenants per IPN event**. `_unit_by_canonical` loads **all units** per unmatched ref. Acknowledged in comments; fine at current scale, will not hold.
- `recalculate_all_statuses` issues 2 queries per unit in a loop, run synchronously from an HTTP cron trigger.
- Reports iterate payments in Python (`MonthlyCollectionReportView`, `TenantPaymentHistoryView`) rather than aggregating in SQL.
- `statement_service._build_ledger` loads a tenant's entire history unbounded and computes the running balance in Python.
- Cron jobs run **synchronously in an HTTP request** (`cron_views.py` — `.apply().get()`); month-end across a growing portfolio will exceed the platform request timeout, leaving jobs half-complete with no resumption.

**Reliability**
- ✅ `PostingFailure` + `retry_posting_failures` is a genuinely good pattern.
- ✅ `_safe_enqueue` correctly prevents broker outages from breaking the bank ack.
- ❌ No alerting on unresolved `PostingFailure` rows — the mechanism records problems nobody is told about (and C3 generates them silently).
- ⚠ `poll_bank_statement` — the documented safety net for IPN downtime — is **a stub** (`tasks.py:277-304`). If the endpoint is down past Co-op's retry window, those credits are lost with no backfill. This is the single largest operational gap.
- ⚠ No monitoring of the reconciliation gap: nothing compares total bank credits to total recorded payments.

## Monetary Precision

Mostly good, with real leaks:
- ✅ `Decimal` end-to-end in models, services, `tax_service`, `posting`. No float arithmetic in the money path. `ROUND_HALF_UP` used consistently. `split_tax_inclusive` correctly makes VAT absorb the rounding so `net + tax == gross` exactly.
- ❌ **`views_reports.py` casts every monetary value to `float()`** before serialising — the P&L, trial balance, arrears report, expense breakdown, and balance sheet all return floats. Precision loss and inconsistent rounding in the numbers presented as the books.
- ❌ `_net_income_sum` (`views_reports.py:35-45`) divides by `1.16` **in SQL**, while `split_tax_inclusive` quantizes with `ROUND_HALF_UP` in Python. The payment-derived P&L and the ledger-derived P&L will differ by cents per commercial transaction and will not tie out.
- ⚠ `TrialBalanceView` tolerates a 0.01 imbalance (`views_reports.py:278`); with correct double-entry it should be exactly zero.
- ⚠ `OccupancyHistoryView` and `ExpenseBreakdownReportView` compute percentages in float.

- `get_collection_progress` (`services.py:308-331`): `expected` sums `monthly_rent` for **all** active tenants portfolio-wide, `collected` sums **all** payments including deposits and late fees, with no building scope and no VAT handling. Percentage can exceed 100 and does not reconcile with either the P&L or the arrears report.

## Final Recommendation

### Requires Significant Changes

Do not treat this system as the book of record until the Critical tier is closed. It is safe to keep running as an *operational* dashboard (rent roll, reminders, tenant records) provided nobody relies on `Arrears`, the P&L, or the balance sheet for money decisions — but that is precisely what the accounting direction calls for it to become.

**Recommended sequence:**

**Phase 1 — stop the bleeding (days, not weeks)**
1. Delete or `DEBUG`-gate `/api/payments/mock/` (C6). One line, removes a money-minting endpoint.
2. Scope `idempotency_key` per tenant and return `409` on mismatch (C1). Stops silent payment loss.
3. Filter `_update_arrears` by `payment_type` (C4). Small change, restores meaning to every arrears figure.
4. Alert on unresolved `PostingFailure` rows. Makes C3-class failures visible immediately.

**Phase 2 — restore the books (1–2 weeks)**
5. Fix the commercial VAT convention end to end, with a reconciliation test as the acceptance criterion (C2).
6. Replace negative-payment voids with a proper reversal model using the existing `reverse_payment` (C3).
7. Introduce a credit balance and carry it forward (H1).
8. Relieve 1040 on payment, or stop raising it (H4).
9. Fix waiver and deposit handling in the statement (H2, H3).

**Phase 3 — make it auditable and defensible (1 week)**
10. Add `role` + DRF permission classes; gate waive/assign/create (C5).
11. Add `created_by` and an append-only change log on all financial models (H7).
12. Add HMAC signature + timestamp validation to the IPN endpoint; populate the IP allowlist (H6).
13. Move Celery off eager; move cron off synchronous HTTP (H8).
14. Implement `poll_bank_statement` — this is the only protection against losing credits during an outage.

**Before go-live, add one non-negotiable control:** a daily automated reconciliation that compares total bank credits received against total payments recorded and total GL cash debits, and alerts on any variance. Every defect above would have been caught on day one by that single check.

# Remediation — applied 1 August 2026

Branch `fix/financial-integrity-audit`, off `dev`. **402 tests pass** (354 pre-existing, 48 new); Ruff clean; no missing migrations.

## What changed

| Finding | Fix |
|---|---|
| **C1** | `Payment.idempotency_key` unique constraint is now scoped `(tenant, idempotency_key)`. A replayed key whose amount or date differs raises `IdempotencyConflict` → **HTTP 409** with the existing payment's id, instead of returning another tenant's record. |
| **C2** | New `Arrears.expected_vat`. The obligation is `expected_rent + expected_vat`, so VAT-inclusive cash is measured against a VAT-inclusive obligation. FIFO now sizes chunks against the true balance and no longer spills VAT into the next period. |
| **C3** | Voids are a flag (`voided_at` / `voided_by` / `void_reason`) plus a mirror-image reversal entry via the existing `reverse_payment`. Negative payments are gone. `payment_type` is preserved, so a voided deposit reverses 1030/2100, not 4110. New owner-only `POST /api/payments/{id}/void/`. |
| **C4** | Only non-void `RENT` settles rent (`SETTLES_RENT`). Applied consistently in `_update_arrears`, `recalculate_all_statuses` and `get_collection_progress`. |
| **C5** | `User.role` (OWNER / ACCOUNTANT / CARETAKER / VIEWER) with `CanRecordMoney` and `CanForgiveMoney`. Recording ≠ forgiving: waive and void are owner-only. New accounts default to VIEWER; existing accounts are promoted to OWNER by migration so nobody is locked out on deploy. |
| **C6** | `/api/payments/mock/` returns 404 unless `DEBUG`. |
| **H1** | `Arrears.credit_applied` + `available_credit()` / `apply_available_credit()`. `generate_monthly_arrears` draws banked credit down against the new period, so a prepaying tenant is no longer billed and dunned. |
| **H2** | Waivers are credited on the statement ledger, so written-off debt stops reappearing. |
| **H3** | Deposits are excluded from the statement's rent ledger — they show only on the "Security Deposit" line instead of also reducing rent owed. |
| **H5** | Reversal matching is now an **exact token** lookup across **all** history, replacing an unanchored substring scan over the newest 500 events. Also moved out of the IPN transaction. |
| **H7** | `FinancialAuditLog` (append-only, read-only in admin) records payment create/void, waivers and credit assignment with actor, old and new values. `Payment.created_by` added. `PaymentAdmin` is now view-only — correction is via void, not silent edits. |

**Also fixed:** the duplicated assign-credit flow is now one service (`assign_unmatched_credit`) used by both API and admin, with locking, status re-check and an idempotency key — the admin copy previously had none; income is reported on one basis everywhere (dashboard trend, annual report and P&L agreed to differ before); voided payments are excluded from all reports, statements and the tenant list; the SMS fallback no longer logs recipient and body; `TenantPaymentHistoryView` 404s instead of 500s; `recalculate_unit_status` accepts the true obligation so commercial tenants can reach `OCCUPIED_PAID`.

## Data migrations

Three, all reversible:

1. `accounts.0005` — promotes existing users to OWNER.
2. `payments.0016` — recomputes **every** arrears row on the corrected basis (adds VAT, drops non-rent payments from `amount_paid`). Historical commercial periods that were cleared 16% early will correctly reopen.
3. `payments.0017` — converts legacy negative `VOID:` payments onto the void flag and re-runs the recompute.

**After deploying, run `python manage.py retry_posting_failures`** to post reversal entries that previously died as `PostingFailure` rows, then review anything still open.

**Expect arrears figures to move.** Commercial balances rise by the VAT that was previously ignored; any period cleared by a deposit reopens. That is the correction, not a regression — brief the owner before deploy.

## Correction to the original audit

The report listed under Database Findings that `ledger.signals` writing `PostingFailure` inside the caller's transaction risked losing the failure record on rollback. That is not a defect: if the outer transaction rolls back, the source row is gone too, so there is nothing left to reconcile. No change was made.

## Deliberately not done

- **H4 — Accounts Receivable (1040) is never relieved.** Utility charges raise a receivable on billing but payments credit income rather than clearing 1040, so the balance grows without bound and rent income is overstated by the utility portion. Fixing it means allocating payments across charge types (rent vs. utility), which changes income recognition and what tenant statements say. That is an accounting-policy decision for the accountant, especially with the COA auditor-locked — not one to make inside a bug-fix branch.
- **H6 — webhook HMAC and replay window.** Requires Co-op to specify a signing scheme. The IP allowlist (`COOP_IPN_ALLOWED_IPS`) should be populated as soon as they share their range; it still defaults to allow-all.
- **H8 — eager Celery and synchronous cron.** Infrastructure: needs Redis and a worker provisioned.
- **Float precision in reports** and **pagination**. Both change the API response contract and would break the existing frontend; they need a coordinated frontend change.
- **`poll_bank_statement` backfill** remains a stub — still the largest operational gap, and still the only protection against losing credits during an IPN outage.

## Recommended next control

Unchanged and still the highest-value single item: a daily automated reconciliation comparing total bank credits received, total payments recorded, and total GL cash debits, alerting on any variance.
